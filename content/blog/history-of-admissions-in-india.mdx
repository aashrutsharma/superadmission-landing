India's admission system did not appear overnight. It has changed step by step over more than 60 years. This post covers how entrance exams and admission authorities evolved, from the first IIT exam to today's national tests and digital records.
The timeline of admissions
1961: The IIT entrance exam starts.
2002: AIEEE begins.
2013: JEE Main and JEE Advanced begin. JEE Main replaced AIEEE.
2015: JoSAA begins. DigiLocker also comes in this year.
2016: NEET begins.
2017: The National Testing Agency (NTA) is set up.
2020: The National Education Policy (NEP) is approved.
2021: The Academic Bank of Credits launches.
2022: CUET UG starts.
2024: The APAAR student ID launches.
2026: JoSAA runs counselling for 138 institutes.
These dates come from the IITs, CBSE, NTA, JoSAA and the Ministry of Education.
4 big changes that shaped admissions in India
1. Common entrance exams. Common national tests like JEE Main, NEET and CUET UG now do the job of many separate tests.
2. One body to run the tests. The National Testing Agency (NTA) was set up in 2017. Before that, CBSE ran JEE Main and NEET.
3. One counselling process for many colleges. Counselling bodies such as JoSAA and MCC were set up to run counselling for multiple colleges together. A student no longer has to deal with each college separately.
4. Digital records. DigiLocker came in 2015, the Academic Bank of Credits in 2021 and the APAAR student ID in 2024. These hold government-issued documents and academic records in digital form.
The numbers grew too
More students now take the exam that leads to engineering admissions.
2013: 12.82 lakh candidates registered for JEE Main. This was the first year of JEE Main, which replaced AIEEE.
2024: 14.77 lakh registered for JEE Main.
2025: 15.40 lakh registered for JEE Main.
2026: 16,04,854 unique candidates registered for JEE Main. This is a significant rise from 2013.
Across all higher education, enrolment grew by over 30% between 2014-15 and 2023-24. That is from 3.42 crore to 4.50 crore students.
The National Education Policy (NEP) 2020 aims to raise India's Higher Education Gross Enrolment Ratio (GER) to 50% by 2035. This expansion intends to add 3.5 crore new seats in higher education.
