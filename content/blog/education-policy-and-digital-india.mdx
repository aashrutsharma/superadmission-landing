India's admission system is changing because of a national education plan and a set of digital tools. This post explains NEP 2020 and the digital building blocks that link students, documents and colleges.
NEP 2020: the foundation for everything else
The [National Education Policy (NEP)](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/userfiles/NEP_Final_English_0.pdf) was approved on 29 July 2020. It replaced a policy that was 34 years old.
Goal: more students in college. The policy aims for a GER of 50% by 2035. GER means Gross Enrolment Ratio. If 100 young people are aged 18 to 23 and 30 are in college, the GER is about 30.
Common entrance tests. The policy asks NTA to offer common entrance exams for university admissions. CUET UG started in 2022.
Credits you can carry. Students can save study credits in the Academic Bank of Credits. It launched on 29 July 2021.
Easier to move between courses. The policy supports flexible study paths, so a student is not stuck in one track.
How many students are in college?
Higher education enrolment grew from 3.42 crore in 2014-15 to 4.50 crore in 2023-24. One crore is 10 million. The source is the AISHE reports by the Ministry of Education.
How close is India to the goal?
The gross enrolment ratio went from 23.7 in 2014-15 to 30.0 in 2023-24. The NEP 2020 goal is 50 by 2035.
Think of it this way. Out of 100 young people aged 18 to 23, about 30 are in college today. The goal is 50.
Digital India tools
Four tools are linked to each other: Aadhaar, DigiLocker, APAAR and the Academic Bank of Credits.
Aadhaar. Aadhaar is a secure, unique 12-digit identification number issued by the Indian government to its residents. It uses fingerprints and iris scans to prove who you are. This makes it easier to use government benefits and tools.
DigiLocker: where verified documents live. DigiLocker is a digital storage system. Official documents like board marksheets and certificates, and sometimes Aadhaar itself, are placed there directly by the organisation that issued them. They are digitally signed, and students can share them whenever they are asked for.
APAAR: one ID for a student's entire academic life. APAAR stands for Automated Permanent Academic Account Registry. It gives each student a 12-digit lifelong ID, often called One Nation, One Student ID. It launched on 13 February 2024 and links to Aadhaar, DigiLocker and the Academic Bank of Credits. Think of it as an ID number, similar in spirit to Aadhaar, but built to follow a student's academic record.
ABC: a bank account for academic credits. The Academic Bank of Credits works like its name suggests, except what gets deposited is course credits, not money. It is the direct technical answer to NEP's promise of multiple entry and exit. Say a student studies for a semester at one college and later moves to a different one. Or say she takes a certificate course alongside her degree. ABC stores the credits earned along the way in one place, tied to her APAAR ID. Credit earned at one institution does not vanish when she is no longer enrolled there. She can carry it forward, and in principle redeem it toward a future degree elsewhere. It launched on 29 July 2021.
NDEAR: the core rules. NDEAR stands for National Digital Education Architecture. It is not a product a student ever touches directly. It is the set of standards that APAAR, ABC, DigiLocker and other education systems agree to follow. This lets them exchange information without every pair of systems needing a custom-built connection. A useful way to think about it: NDEAR is the agreed shape of the pipes. APAAR, ABC and DigiLocker are what flows through those pipes.
DPDP: Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023
The DPDP Act sets out a few plain obligations for anyone handling personal data. This includes Unnati's rank, category, documents and contact details as they move through counselling.
Consent is required. It has to be specific, not buried in fine print she never reads.
Data can only be used for the purpose it was collected for. A counselling body that collects her category for seat allocation cannot reuse it for something unrelated without asking again.
She has a right to correction and erasure. If something about her stored data is wrong, or she wants it removed once it is no longer needed, the law gives her a route to ask for that.
Any system that proposes to move a student's data between counselling bodies, DigiLocker and APAAR, the way Superadmission proposes to, has to be built around these obligations.
The IndiaAI Mission
The IndiaAI Mission is the government's national program for building India's AI capability. It covers compute infrastructure, shared datasets and skilling. It also has a focus area called Safe and Trusted AI. This is about making sure AI systems used in important decisions are explainable and do not behave like an unaccountable black box.
This applies directly to PraveshAI, the reasoning and guidance engine inside Superadmission. A seat allocation decision affects a student's entire future, so an unexplainable black box AI is a major problem. PraveshAI is built so its logic can be inspected and reasoned about. This matches what the Safe and Trusted AI pillar of the IndiaAI Mission asks for.
Verified digital records can take the place of paper copies. That can save students time at every step.
