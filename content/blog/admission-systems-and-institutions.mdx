Three kinds of bodies take part in admissions in India. Each one has a different job.
Exam bodies run the exam. They give a score and a rank.
Counselling bodies match ranks to seats.
Colleges teach the students and give the degree.
Exam bodies
NTA. The National Testing Agency runs JEE Main, NEET UG and CUET UG. It does not run any college. It does not decide who gets a seat.
The IITs. One IIT runs JEE Advanced each year, in turns. Students qualify for it through JEE Main.
State exam bodies. Many states run their own entrance tests, called CETs, for seats in that state.
Private universities. Some private universities run their own entrance tests.
Counselling bodies
JoSAA: IITs, NITs, IIITs and other government funded technical institutes. 138 institutes in 2026.
CSAB: Extra rounds for NITs, IIITs and other technical institutes after JoSAA.
MCC: The Medical Counselling Committee. It runs counselling for many MBBS and BDS seats.
State authorities: Each state runs counselling for its own seats.
In all, there are 40+ counselling bodies in India.
Colleges
India has 59,533 institutions in the latest national survey, AISHE 2023-24, by the Ministry of Education. These are the institutions that responded to the survey. They split like this:
Colleges: 46,468
Standalone institutions: 11,787
Universities: 1,278
What each one means:
University: Gives degrees and has multiple colleges under it and affiliated to it.
College: Usually joined to a university. The university gives the degree.
Standalone: Not a university or a college of one. Examples are polytechnics and nursing schools.
Kinds of institutions you will hear about
IIT: Indian Institute of Technology. Top government engineering institutes. 23 take part in JoSAA.
NIT: National Institute of Technology. Government engineering institutes. 31 take part in JoSAA.
IIIT: Indian Institute of Information Technology. Focus on computers and IT. 26 take part in JoSAA.
GFTI: Government Funded Technical Institute. Other technical institutes the government funds. 56 take part in JoSAA in 2026.
Central university: Set up by an Act of Parliament and funded by the Central Government. Many use CUET UG.
State university: Set up by a state law and funded by the state.
Deemed university: An institution given university status by the Central Government. It keeps its own special focus.
Private university: Set up by a state law but run by a private body, not by the government.
Who makes the rules?
UGC: University Grants Commission. Looks after universities.
AICTE: All India Council for Technical Education. Looks after technical education.
NMC: National Medical Commission. Looks after medical education. It replaced the Medical Council of India in 2020.
Not every seat is filled through the big counselling bodies. Some colleges fill some seats through their own process.
